\begin{abstract}
Accurate segmentation of plant structures in field and greenhouse imagery is a
prerequisite for high-throughput, automated phenotyping.  Existing tools either
demand substantial computational resources, require command-line expertise, or
are limited to a single segmentation paradigm.  We present \textbf{LeafSegmenter},
an open-source, cross-platform desktop application that combines the
state-of-the-art Segment Anything Model~2 (SAM2) with a suite of interactive
mask-editing, image-enhancement, and model-training tools accessible to
non-expert users on standard laptop hardware.  The application supports both
GPU-accelerated inference (CUDA and Apple MPS) and fully CPU-based operation,
making it accessible without high-performance computing infrastructure.
Key capabilities include an interactive colour-based mask filter, geometric and
species-specific leaf-completion templates, a leaf-unfolding module based on
mirror reflection across the detected fold line, an undo history system, and a
training pipeline that allows users to distil their curated SAM2 masks into a
lightweight U-Net model for rapid, automatic deployment on new images.
Comprehensive phenotyping metrics (area, length, width, shape descriptors,
colour statistics, and vegetation indices) are exported as structured CSV files.
LeafSegmenter lowers the barrier to entry for plant phenotyping research
while remaining extensible to heavier architectures and novel species.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} plant phenotyping, image segmentation, Segment Anything
Model, vision transformer, interactive tools, leaf morphology, field imaging.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Plant phenotyping — the quantitative measurement of plant morphology, colour,
and structural traits — is central to modern crop improvement programmes and
fundamental plant biology research \citep{walter2015plant, furbank2011phenomics}.
The increasing accessibility of digital cameras and unmanned aerial vehicles
has generated vast quantities of plant imagery from both field trials and
controlled greenhouse environments, yet the bottleneck in extracting meaningful
measurements has shifted from image acquisition to accurate, scalable
\textit{segmentation}: the process of delineating individual plant structures
from their backgrounds and from one another \citep{pound2017deep}.

\subsection{Segmentation in Plant Science}

Instance segmentation — assigning a distinct mask to each individual object
(leaf, stem, fruit) in an image — remains one of the most challenging tasks in
agricultural computer vision.  Leaves overlap, lighting conditions vary
dramatically between field and greenhouse settings, colour contrasts between
plant tissue and background are often subtle, and plant morphology varies
substantially across growth stages, genotypes, and stress conditions
\citep{singh2016machine, jiang2020deepleaf}.  Early approaches relied on
classical colour thresholding and morphological operations \citep{aksoy2015use},
which perform well only under tightly controlled conditions.  The advent of
deep learning, and convolutional neural networks in particular, substantially
advanced segmentation quality \citep{mohanty2016using, sa2016deepfruits}, but
these approaches require large annotated datasets and significant computational
resources, limiting their adoption outside well-funded research groups.

\subsection{Foundation Models and the Segment Anything Model}

A transformative development in computer vision has been the emergence of
large-scale \textit{foundation models}: neural networks pre-trained on
internet-scale datasets that generalise remarkably well to novel domains with
little or no additional fine-tuning \citep{bommasani2021opportunities}.  For
image segmentation, Meta AI Research released the Segment Anything Model
(SAM) in 2023 \citep{kirillov2023segment}, followed by the substantially
improved SAM~2 in 2024 \citep{ravi2024sam2}.

SAM~2 employs a hierarchical Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone
\citep{dosovitskiy2020image} coupled with a streaming memory module originally
designed for video object segmentation.  The ViT backbone divides the input
image into fixed-size patches, encodes them into high-dimensional token
representations, and processes them through multi-head self-attention layers
that capture long-range spatial dependencies without the inductive bias of
convolutions \citep{vaswani2017attention, wang2022pvt}.  The result is a
powerful, promptable segmentation model: given point, box, or mask prompts
from a user, SAM~2 generates high-quality instance masks for arbitrary object
categories.  Critically, SAM~2 operates in a zero-shot regime — it requires no
training on plant-specific data to segment leaves, stems, or fruits
\citep{ravi2024sam2}.

Several studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of SAM and SAM~2 for
agricultural applications, including crop row detection, fruit counting, and
leaf instance segmentation \citep{wu2023samplant, wang2023samag}.  However,
direct use of SAM~2 in a research workflow requires Python programming
knowledge, access to the model weights (up to 2.4~GB), and familiarity with
its API, presenting a significant barrier for plant biologists without
computational training.

\subsection{Vision Transformers and Lightweight Alternatives}

While full ViT-based models such as SAM~2 deliver state-of-the-art
segmentation quality, their inference cost can be prohibitive on standard
laptop hardware.  Encoder–decoder architectures based on U-Net
\citep{ronneberger2015unet} paired with lightweight ImageNet-pre-trained
convolutional encoders (ResNet~\citep{he2016deep}, EfficientNet
\citep{tan2019efficientnet}) offer a practical trade-off: substantially faster
inference with moderate accuracy loss.  MobileSAM \citep{zhang2023mobilesam},
a distilled variant of SAM trained to replicate its image encoder using a
lightweight ViT-Tiny backbone, achieves segmentation quality close to the full
SAM while reducing model size by over 60-fold, making deployment on CPU
feasible.

The knowledge-distillation paradigm — training a compact \textit{student} model
to reproduce the outputs of a powerful \textit{teacher} model
\citep{hinton2015distilling} — forms the conceptual basis of the training
pipeline in the present work: users collect high-quality masks generated by
SAM~2, then use those masks to supervise training of a lightweight U-Net for
rapid, class-specific, fully automatic deployment.

\subsection{Motivation and Scope}

Despite the power of modern segmentation models, accessible, interactive
tooling for plant phenotyping remains scarce.  Tools such as
LabelMe~\citep{russell2008labelme}, CVAT, and Roboflow provide general-purpose
annotation interfaces but lack plant-specific enhancement pipelines,
phenotype measurement modules, or integrated model-training workflows.
PlantCV~\citep{fahlgren2015plantcv} offers a comprehensive plant image analysis
library but operates through Python scripts rather than a graphical interface.
iMeasure and similar tools support morphometric measurements but not
deep-learning-based segmentation.

We therefore developed \textbf{LeafSegmenter}, an open-source desktop
application that addresses all of the following requirements simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item GPU-optional operation: full functionality on laptop CPU, with
          optional acceleration via CUDA (NVIDIA) or Apple MPS (Apple Silicon).
    \item Interactive, SAM2-based segmentation with no coding required.
    \item A rich suite of image-enhancement, mask-editing, and
          phenotype-measurement tools designed specifically for plant imagery.
    \item An integrated training pipeline enabling users to convert curated
          SAM~2 masks into a deployable lightweight model.
    \item Novel tools for geometric leaf completion and leaf-unfolding
          reconstruction, extending the utility of partial or occluded masks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Materials and Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Application Architecture}

LeafSegmenter is implemented in Python~3.11 using the \texttt{tkinter} toolkit
for the graphical interface.  The application is structured as a modular
package (\texttt{app/}) comprising seven functional modules:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{gui\_app.py} — main application class, layout, event handling
    \item \texttt{image\_processing.py} — pure image enhancement functions
    \item \texttt{mask\_utils.py} — mask mathematics and shape completion geometry
    \item \texttt{phenotyping.py} — phenotype measurement and CSV export
    \item \texttt{sam2\_utils.py} — SAM~2 model loading and Hydra configuration
    \item \texttt{color\_filter.py} — interactive colour-based mask filtering
    \item \texttt{tab\_leaf\_completion.py}, \texttt{tab\_leaf\_unfolding.py},
          \texttt{tab\_train.py} — training panel tab modules
\end{itemize}

The modular design ensures that computationally intensive functions (image
enhancement, mask geometry, phenotype measurement) are decoupled from the GUI
layer and can be invoked independently for batch processing or unit testing.

\subsection{Image Loading and Preprocessing}

The application accepts TIFF, PNG, JPEG, and BMP images.  A built-in TIF
converter supports conversion of raw field-acquisition formats to standard
8-bit PNG prior to segmentation.  Images are loaded as RGB \texttt{uint8}
NumPy arrays via Pillow and OpenCV.

A rotation module supports arbitrary angle rotation via a dial widget with
preset shortcuts (45°, 90°, 180°, 270°, $-$45°, $-$90°), allowing correct
orientation of images acquired at non-standard angles before segmentation.

\subsection{Image Enhancement Pipeline}

A dedicated enhancement panel provides a sequential processing pipeline
designed for plant imagery.  The pipeline is configurable through the GUI and
includes the following operations:

\subsubsection{Green-Channel CLAHE Enhancement}

The primary plant-specific enhancement applies Contrast Limited Adaptive
Histogram Equalisation (CLAHE) \citep{zuiderveld1994clahe} exclusively to the
value channel (V) of pixels identified as green by HSV thresholding.  This
selectively improves contrast within leaf tissue while leaving background
regions unaffected, improving the signal-to-background ratio for SAM~2.
A bilateral filter \citep{tomasi1998bilateral} is subsequently applied to
reduce noise while preserving leaf edges, followed by Sobel edge blending
\citep{sobel1968isotropic} to further accentuate boundary regions.  An unsharp
masking step \citep{dahl2008contrast} completes the standard plant pipeline.

\subsubsection{Vegetation Indices}

The application computes five widely used vegetation indices from the RGB
channels, which may be used either as visual enhancement overlays or as
input features for downstream analysis:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ExG} (Excess Green): $\mathrm{ExG} = 2G - R - B$
          \citep{woebbecke1995color}
    \item \textbf{ExGR}: $\mathrm{ExGR} = \mathrm{ExG} - 1.4R - G$
    \item \textbf{GRVI} (Green-Red Vegetation Index):
          $\mathrm{GRVI} = (G - R)/(G + R)$ \citep{tucker1979red}
    \item \textbf{VARI} (Visible Atmospherically Resistant Index):
          $\mathrm{VARI} = (G - R)/(G + R - B)$ \citep{gitelson2002novel}
    \item \textbf{GLI} (Green Leaf Index):
          $\mathrm{GLI} = (2G - R - B)/(2G + R + B)$ \citep{louhaichi2001spatially}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Advanced Enhancement Options}

Additional enhancement methods are available for challenging imaging
conditions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Retinex} (single- and multi-scale) \citep{jobson1997multiscale}
          for illumination normalisation under variable field lighting.
    \item \textbf{Morphological top-hat transform} \citep{serra1983image} for
          uneven background illumination correction.
    \item \textbf{Non-local means denoising} \citep{buades2005non} for
          high-noise images.
    \item \textbf{Guided filtering} \citep{he2013guided} for edge-preserving
          smoothing using the green channel as guide.
    \item \textbf{LAB colour-space enhancement}, \textbf{grey-world and
          max-white white balance}, \textbf{Difference of Gaussians},
          \textbf{local contrast normalisation}, \textbf{adaptive gamma
          correction}, and \textbf{shadow/highlight recovery}.
    \item \textbf{Background whitening}: HSV-based detection of
          bright, desaturated background pixels (e.g.\ white imaging trays)
          with morphological cleanup, replacing them with white to simplify
          foreground segmentation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{SAM2-Based Segmentation}

LeafSegmenter integrates SAM~2 \citep{ravi2024sam2} via the official Python
API.  The user specifies a SAM~2 checkpoint (Hiera-Small, Hiera-Large, etc.\
or a bundled \texttt{.pt} file) and the target compute device (CPU, CUDA, or
Apple MPS).  Segmentation is triggered on the current image after enhancement,
producing a set of instance masks via \texttt{SAM2AutomaticMaskGenerator} with
fully configurable hyperparameters exposed in the GUI:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{points\_per\_side}: grid density of prompt points.
    \item \texttt{pred\_iou\_thresh}: predicted IoU quality threshold.
    \item \texttt{stability\_score\_thresh}: mask stability rejection threshold.
    \item \texttt{crop\_n\_layers}: number of multi-scale crop passes.
    \item \texttt{crop\_overlap\_ratio}: overlap between adjacent crops.
    \item \texttt{box\_nms\_thresh}: duplicate suppression threshold.
    \item \texttt{min\_mask\_region\_area}: minimum mask area in pixels.
    \item \texttt{use\_m2m}: mask-to-mask refinement flag.
\end{itemize}

Hover tooltips in the GUI provide plain-language explanations of each
parameter.  After generation, masks are deduplicated by Intersection-over-Union
(IoU $> 0.80$) and split into single-connected-component instances.  Batch
segmentation processes an entire folder, caching results in memory for
subsequent review and export.

\subsection{Mask Editing Tools}

A dedicated Masks panel provides interactive editing operations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Delete}: remove selected masks from the set.
    \item \textbf{Combine}: union of two or more selected masks into one.
    \item \textbf{Refine}: re-run SAM~2 within the bounding box of a selected
          mask using denser prompting for improved boundary accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Edit boundary}: an interactive contour editor allowing
          point-by-point drag adjustment of mask boundaries, with add-point
          (Ctrl+click) and delete-point (Shift+click) operations and optional
          Gaussian smoothing.
    \item \textbf{Split}: decompose multi-blob masks into individual
          connected-component masks.
\end{itemize}

An undo history system (up to 50 states) captures the full mask list before
each destructive operation, enabling recovery via Ctrl+Z (or Cmd+Z on macOS).

\subsection{Colour-Based Mask Filtering}

A colour filter panel provides interactive mask selection based on pixel
colour statistics.  An interactive two-dimensional Hue $\times$ Saturation
picker renders the full HSV colour space as a gradient.  Users drag
rectangles on the picker to define one or more colour ranges; these are
combined with OR logic.  An eyedropper tool samples a pixel directly from the
preview canvas, placing a crosshair marker on the picker at the sampled
position and automatically generating a selection rectangle with a sensible
tolerance.  A separate brightness (Value) range control applies globally across
all selections.  The filter operates on the mean HSV colour of each mask's
constituent pixels, allowing rapid removal of soil, background, or specular
highlight masks without manual selection.

\subsection{Leaf Shape Completion}
\label{sec:completion}

Many plant images contain partially occluded or truncated leaves.  To support
reconstruction of the complete leaf area for morphometric analysis, LeafSegmenter
includes a leaf completion module offering two complementary shape galleries:

\subsubsection{Geometric Shape Gallery}

Eighteen botanical leaf forms are available (Orbicular, Elliptical, Ovate,
Obovate, Cordate, Reniform, Lanceolate, Oblanceolate, Oblong, Linear,
Ensiform, Cuneate, Spathulate, Deltoid, Rhomboid, Sagittate, Hastate, and a
general Ellipse), each rendered as a silhouette thumbnail using parametric
polygon definitions.

\subsubsection{Crop Species Gallery}

Eighteen crop-specific silhouettes are provided, traced from documented leaf
morphology: Maize, Wheat, Sorghum, Rice, Barley (monocots with narrow strap
leaves), Arabidopsis (rosette form), Tomato and Potato (pinnate compound),
Soybean (trifoliate), Sunflower, Tobacco, Cassava (palmate), Cotton, Grape,
Common Bean, Cucumber, Pepper, and Lettuce.

\subsubsection{Alignment Algorithm}

Given a selected mask and shape template, the completion algorithm performs
three sequential alignment steps:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Centroid alignment}: the shape template is translated so that
          its centre coincides with the pixel centre of mass (centroid) of the
          mask.
    \item \textbf{PCA orientation}: Principal Component Analysis is applied to
          the mask pixel coordinates.  The major eigenvector defines the leaf's
          primary axis; the template is rotated to align its major axis with
          this direction.
    \item \textbf{Scale fitting}: the template axes are scaled to match the
          PCA-derived major and minor axis lengths of the mask.
\end{enumerate}

The aligned template is rasterised at full image resolution and merged with
the original mask (preserving all original pixels).  Fine adjustments to
scale, position (drag), and rotation angle are available interactively before
the completed mask is committed.

\subsection{Leaf Unfolding Reconstruction}
\label{sec:unfolding}

Folded leaves are common in greenhouse and field imagery, particularly in
maize, sorghum, and wheat.  A dedicated Leaf Unfolding module reconstructs
the complete leaf by mirroring the folded portion across the detected fold
line.

The fold line is detected automatically as the junction boundary between two
selected masks (representing the two halves of the folded leaf).  Both masks
are dilated morphologically and their intersection defines the junction zone.
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is applied to the junction pixel
coordinates, with the first right singular vector defining the fold line
direction.  Each pixel $\mathbf{p}$ of the folded mask is then reflected as:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}' = \mathbf{p} - 2\left[(\mathbf{p} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) \cdot
    \hat{\mathbf{n}}\right]\hat{\mathbf{n}}
    \label{eq:reflection}
\end{equation}

where $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is a point on the fold line and $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$
is the unit normal to the fold line direction.  The reflected mask is
morphologically closed to fill rounding artefacts, and the corresponding image
pixels are mapped to their reflected positions.  The user may fine-tune the
fold line angle ($\pm$5° increments) and shift the mirrored piece interactively
before applying.

\subsection{Phenotype Measurement and Export}

For each mask (or set of selected masks), the following measurements are
computed and exported as CSV files:

\subsubsection{Morphometric Descriptors}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Area} (px$^2$)
    \item \textbf{Length and width}: PCA major/minor axis lengths; deskewed
          row-span length and 95th-percentile row width.
    \item \textbf{Perimeter}, \textbf{convex hull area and perimeter}
    \item \textbf{Solidity}: mask area / convex hull area
    \item \textbf{Circularity}: $4\pi A / P^2$
    \item \textbf{Extent}: mask area / bounding box area
    \item \textbf{Equivalent diameter}: diameter of a circle of equal area
    \item \textbf{Connected components}: number of distinct blobs
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Colour Descriptors}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Per-channel (R, G, B, H, S, V) mean, median, sum, and standard
          deviation within the mask
    \item HSV channel variances
    \item Vegetation index means: ExG, ExR, ExGR, GLI, green pixel fraction
\end{itemize}

Both \textit{individual} (per-segment) and \textit{joint} (aggregated across
a selection) CSV exports are supported.  Masks may also be saved as full-size
binary PNG files, bounding-box-cropped binary masks, and RGBA transparent-background
crops.

\subsection{Custom Model Training}
\label{sec:training}

To enable rapid, class-specific segmentation without SAM~2 at inference time,
LeafSegmenter includes an integrated training pipeline.

\subsubsection{Data Collection}

Users collect training examples through normal SAM~2-assisted segmentation:
after segmenting an image, selected masks are added to a structured dataset
(images in \texttt{images/}, binary masks in \texttt{masks/}) via a single
button press.  Images may also be marked as negative examples (no target
present).

\subsubsection{Model Architecture}

The default architecture is a U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} with a
ResNet-18 \citep{he2016deep} encoder pre-trained on ImageNet
\citep{deng2009imagenet}, implemented via the
\texttt{segmentation-models-pytorch} library \citep{yakubovskiy2019smp}.
A lightweight alternative (\texttt{unet\_small}) omits the pre-trained encoder
for resource-constrained scenarios.  The binary segmentation decoder produces
a single-channel output mask.

\subsubsection{Training Procedure}

Training uses the Adam optimiser \citep{kingma2014adam} with a configurable
learning rate (default $10^{-5}$), batch size, image size, device, and number
of gradient steps.  A weighted binary cross-entropy loss accounts for class
imbalance between foreground and background pixels.  Training can be resumed
from an existing checkpoint.  All hyperparameters are exposed in the GUI
without requiring code modification.

\subsubsection{Inference}

The trained model accepts arbitrary-resolution images, applies the same
enhancement pipeline as SAM~2 segmentation, and produces instance masks via
connected-component analysis of the thresholded output probability map.  A
sliding-window (tile-based) inference mode handles large images by processing
overlapping tiles and merging predictions.  An optional colour-guided tile
scanning mode restricts tile processing to regions containing plant-coloured
pixels, substantially reducing inference time on large field images.
